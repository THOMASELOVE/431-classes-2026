## The Power, Peril and Pervasiveness of Artificial Intelligence

Friday 2026-10-30 at 9 AM, Rammelkamp R219, 2500 MetroHealth Dr, Cleveland OH 44109 or via Zoom

Thomas E. Love, Ph.D.

Artificial intelligence systems increasingly shape how people access health information, make medical decisions, and receive care. Some now consider AI a "determinant of health". In scientific research, AI is used for literature screening, data extraction, and even to generate research questions and study designs. The potential dangers of AI extend far beyond simple errors or inconveniences, touching upon ethical, societal, academic, and even existential concerns. The potential for AI to revolutionize health research is also immense. Ultimately, AI may well expand our intellectual and statistical modeling capacity, advancing our ability to evaluate complex systems and patient symptoms effectively. But not everything is ready for prime time, and everyone seems to be in such a hurry. 

As Arthur C Clarke so memorably put it: any sufficiently advanced technology is indistinguishable from magic. But it's equally important to remember Clarke's other two laws: (1) The only way of discovering the limits of the possible is to venture a little way past them into the impossible. (2) When a distinguished but elderly scientist states that something is possible, they are almost certainly right. When they state that something is impossible, they are very probably wrong. I beg your indulgence as I attempt to be helpfully wrong, as I briefly explore a range of AI-related topics relevant to health researchers.
